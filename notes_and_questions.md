## Part 1: Detailed Revision Notes

### 1. Foundational Background & The Flow of Genetic Information

* **Central Dogma Framework:** The flow of genetic information outlines how DNA stores hereditary instructions, directing cellular function and continuity.
* **Early Historical Milestones:** Investigations into hereditary material required proving whether proteins or nucleic acids carried genetic instructions. The early isolation of DNA laid the groundwork for structural and functional discoveries.

### 2. Experimental Evidence Identifying DNA as Genetic Material

* **Griffith’s Transformation Experiments (1928):**
* Studied *Streptococcus pneumoniae* strains (smooth encapsulated virulent strains vs. rough non-encapsulated non-virulent strains).
* Demonstrated that heat-killed virulent bacteria could "transform" live non-virulent rough cells into virulent smooth cells, signaling a hereditary transforming principle.


* **In Vitro Transformation Assays:** Follow-up in vitro assays isolated cellular components to pinpoint the specific macromolecule responsible for transformation.
* **Hershey and Chase Experiments (1952):**
* Utilized bacteriophages (viruses infecting bacteria) labeled with radioactive isotopes ($^{32}\text{P}$ for DNA, $^{35}\text{S}$ for protein coats).
* Proved conclusively that viral DNA—not protein—enters host bacterial cells during infection to reprogram them, confirming DNA as the universal genetic material.



### 3. Nucleic Acid Molecular Structure & Composition

* **Monomer Building Blocks:** Nucleic acids are polymers composed of nucleotide subunits consisting of a pentose sugar, a phosphate group, and a nitrogenous base.
* **Nitrogenous Bases:** Divided into purines (Adenine, Guanine) and pyrimidines (Cytosine, Thymine in DNA, Uracil in RNA).
* **Backbone Linkages:** Nucleotides polymerize via covalent **phosphodiester bonds** linking the 3' carbon of one sugar to the 5' carbon of the next via a phosphate group, establishing directionality (5' to 3').
* **Nomenclature:** Individual nucleotide units, such as deoxyadenosine monophosphate, reflect the specific sugar, base, and phosphate makeup.

### Answer Key: Multiple-Choice Questions (MCQs)

* **1. B ($^{32}\text{P}$)**
* *Explanation:* Phosphorus is found in DNA backbones but not in standard proteins, making $^{32}\text{P}$ ideal for labeling viral DNA. Sulfur ($^{35}\text{S}$) was used to label proteins.


* **2. B (Heat-killed virulent bacteria release a "transforming principle" that can alter the phenotype of non-virulent strains.)**
* *Explanation:* Griffith showed that rough non-virulent bacteria took up a transforming factor from heat-killed smooth cells, converting them into virulent strains.


* **3. C (Phosphodiester bond)**
* *Explanation:* Phosphodiester bonds connect adjacent nucleotide sugars, forming the strong covalent sugar-phosphate backbone.


* **4. A (A nitrogenous base and a phosphate group)**
* *Explanation:* A standard mononucleotide consists of a pentose sugar attached to a nitrogenous base (at the 1' position) and one or more phosphate groups (at the 5' position).


* **5. B (Viral protein coat labeled with sulfur)**
* *Explanation:* Hershey and Chase found that the radioactive protein ghosts remained outside the bacterial cell, whereas viral DNA entered the cytoplasm.

### Model Answers: Short Written Answer Questions

* **Model Answer 8:**
Hershey and Chase took advantage of the distinct chemical compositions of DNA and proteins. Proteins contain sulfur ($S$) but virtually no phosphorus, whereas DNA contains phosphorus ($P$) in its sugar-phosphate backbone but no sulfur. By growing bacteriophages in media containing radioactive sulfur ($^{35}\text{S}$) to label proteins or radioactive phosphorus ($^{32}\text{P}$) to label DNA, they tracked which component entered host bacteria during infection. After agitation in a blender and centrifugation, they discovered that $^{32}\text{P}$ (DNA) entered the bacterial cells and was passed on to progeny phages, while $^{35}\text{S}$ (protein) remained outside. This confirmed that DNA is the hereditary material responsible for viral reprogramming.
* **Model Answer 9:**
Phosphodiester bonds are covalent linkages formed between the 5' phosphate group of one nucleotide and the 3' hydroxyl group of the adjacent pentose sugar. This directional linking creates a stable, repeating sugar-phosphate backbone that protects the inner nitrogenous bases. Because synthesis and reading occur relative to these terminal ends, phosphodiester bonds establish strict structural polarity (running from a free 5' phosphate to a free 3' hydroxyl), which is crucial for template-directed replication, transcription, and overall nucleic acid stability.
