RNA now
basic nucleotide difference from DNA = U instead of T, ribose instead of deoxyribose
ribose has OH at 2' carbon, DNA has H there
small structural differences but very different roles

3 main RNA types in bacteria AND eukaryotes
mRNA messenger, transcribed from DNA, carries instructions for protein
sequence corresponds to DNA coding strand except U for T
central dogma DNA to RNA to protein, RNA here usually means mRNA
rRNA ribosomal - part of ribosomes which make proteins
tRNA transfer - brings amino acids during protein synthesis
also microRNAs in eukaryotes for gene regulation. others too but these 3 main types to know

RNA usually single stranded BUT can pair with itself
regions in same strand complementary, fold back and base pair
stems + loops, local double stranded regions in one molecule

why evolve DNA if RNA already worked?
stability!! RNA much less stable
2' OH can attack phosphate in backbone, break phosphodiester linkage / split chain
DNA has H instead, less susceptible
DNA double helix also protects bases inside
DNA better long term store across generations
ancient DNA can survive in bones / frozen material
Richard III bones in car park example, used DNA to help identify
RNA much harder to keep intact in lab. more stable inside cells but still relatively unstable

second reason - information damage
C can spontaneously deaminate -> U (loses amino group)
in DNA, U shouldn't be there so can recognise as damage
uracil DNA glycosylase removes U, repair system restores correct base
in RNA U is normal! can't easily tell genuine U from one produced by damaged C
information could deteriorate over time
using T in DNA helps identify this type of damage

RNA instability can actually be useful for modern gene expression - messages don't have to last forever
but bad choice for permanent inherited information store
RNA world possible, lots to think about re origins of life
